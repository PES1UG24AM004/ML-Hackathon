1. Problem Overview & Pipeline Architecture

PathSnap solves vehicle trajectory map-matching and sequence route reconstruction on low-frequency, noisy GPS data. The system decouples candidate generation (KD-tree top-8 arcs), discriminative candidate classification (6 pure NumPy models), and topological route reconstruction via Hidden Markov Model (HMM) Viterbi dynamic programming.

2. Dataset Understanding & Exploratory Data Analysis

We inspected the 80 training trips (208,417 GPS observations) and 10 student-testing trips (19,160 GPS observations). Projecting each trip into a per-trip Local Azimuthal Equidistant (AEQD) frame centered at the trajectory median preserves exact metric scale and relative angles.

3.2 Individual Model Analysis

3.2.1 Logistic Regression

  • Suitability & Trade-offs: Serves as a fast, convex linear baseline that outputs well-calibrated probabilities directly usable as HMM emissions. Strengths: rapid inference (0.0088s) and zero overfitting (Train F1 0.8758 vs. Test F1 0.8946). Limitations: cannot model nonlinear interactions between perpendicular distance, heading alignment, and local continuity.
  • Configuration & Objective: L2 penalty C=1.0, L-BFGS-B solver, max_iter=60, standardized 16 features. Loss: L2-regularized binary cross-entropy loss.
  • Experimental Results & Generalisation: Test Macro-F1: 0.8946, Top-1 Accuracy: 0.8462, Route Continuity: 0.9167, Log Loss: 0.1357. No overfitting observed; linear decision boundary slightly underfits complex urban intersections compared to MLP.

3.2.2 Gaussian Naive Bayes

  • Suitability & Trade-offs: Provides a lightweight generative probabilistic baseline assuming conditional feature independence given candidate correctness. Strengths: sub-second training (0.815s) and simple closed-form likelihoods. Limitations: strong independence assumption is violated by correlated distance/kinematic features, inflating probability confidence.
  • Configuration & Objective: var_smoothing=1e-6 on standardized features. Loss: Maximum likelihood estimation of class-conditional Gaussian means and variances.
  • Experimental Results & Generalisation: Test Macro-F1: 0.6049, Top-1 Accuracy: 0.8359, Route Continuity: 0.9127, Log Loss: 2.2160. Severe probability miscalibration (Log Loss 2.2160) due to correlated features; unsuitable as an HMM emission model despite decent Top-1 ranking.

3.2.3 Distance-Weighted k-Nearest Neighbors (kNN)

  • Suitability & Trade-offs: Non-parametric instance-based learner capable of capturing arbitrary local decision boundaries in normalized feature space. Strengths: zero training cost (0.013s) and strong Test Macro-F1 (0.8984). Limitations: slow inference (8.6472s for 153k candidates via KD-tree) makes real-time multi-trip API serving impractical.
  • Configuration & Objective: k=15 neighbors, Euclidean metric, inverse-distance weights 1/(d+1e-4), 25,000 indexed training points. Loss: Non-parametric local majority/distance-weighted voting (no iterative loss optimisation).
  • Experimental Results & Generalisation: Test Macro-F1: 0.8984, Top-1 Accuracy: 0.8384, Route Continuity: 0.9090, Log Loss: 0.1688. Mildly higher Log Loss than MLP due to finite-k probability quantization; inference latency is ~90x slower than MLP.

3.2.4 Random Forest Classifier

  • Suitability & Trade-offs: Bagged ensemble of binned CART trees that captures nonlinear feature thresholds and interactions without feature scaling. Strengths: fast training (1.61s), scale-invariant, and strong Test Macro-F1 (0.9020). Limitations: tree-averaged probabilities (Log Loss 0.1115) are slightly less sharp than MLP at intersections.
  • Configuration & Objective: n_estimators=15, max_depth=8, min_samples_leaf=25, max_features=4, 60k bootstrap subsample. Loss: Gini impurity minimization per split across bootstrap-resampled trees.
  • Experimental Results & Generalisation: Test Macro-F1: 0.9020, Top-1 Accuracy: 0.8397, Route Continuity: 0.9177, Log Loss: 0.1115. Well-regularized by depth cap and leaf minimum; generalizes cleanly from CV (0.8818) to Student-Test (0.9020).

3.2.5 Gradient Boosted Trees (GBM)

  • Suitability & Trade-offs: Sequential additive tree ensemble correcting residual log-odds errors on hard candidate pairs near parallel streets. Strengths: strong nonlinear expressiveness and fast inference (0.1678s). Limitations: with 35 shallow trees on an 80k subsample, slightly higher Log Loss (0.1748) and CV variance (+/-0.0257) than Random Forest or MLP.
  • Configuration & Objective: n_estimators=35, learning_rate=0.1, max_depth=4, min_samples_leaf=30, subsample=0.6. Loss: Sequential negative-gradient (binary cross-entropy residual) minimization.
  • Experimental Results & Generalisation: Test Macro-F1: 0.8969, Top-1 Accuracy: 0.8432, Route Continuity: 0.9121, Log Loss: 0.1748. No severe overfitting, though probability calibration lags behind MLP.

3.2.6 Shallow MLP (Neural Network)

  • Suitability & Trade-offs: Learns smooth, continuous nonlinear combinations of spatial distance, heading alignment, and local temporal continuity. Strengths: highest CV Macro-F1 (0.8909), highest Test Macro-F1 (0.9103), highest Top-1 Accuracy (0.8502), lowest Log Loss (0.1027), and fast inference (0.0954s). Limitations: requires feature standardization and ~22s training.
  • Configuration & Objective: 1 hidden layer (64 ReLU units), Adam lr=1e-3, batch_size=8192, epochs=12, weight_decay=1e-4. Loss: L2-regularized binary cross-entropy optimized with Adam.
  • Experimental Results & Generalisation: Test Macro-F1: 0.9103, Top-1 Accuracy: 0.8502, Route Continuity: 0.9203, Log Loss: 0.1027. Epoch-wise train and validation loss curves decrease smoothly together with zero divergence, confirming strong generalization.

7. API, UI and Two-Laptop Deployment

The production system is split across two laptops: Laptop 1 runs the FastAPI backend (src/api/main.py via uvicorn), exposing GET /health, GET /api/trips, and POST /api/match/{trip_id}. Laptop 2 serves the evaluator-facing web UI (src/ui/index.html), which renders the interactive Leaflet route map and live metrics.
